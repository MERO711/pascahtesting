Container, Quimbaya culture

By Global Digital Heritage and GDH-Afrika

The Quimbaya culture was a Pre-Columbian culture in Colombia, famed for their exquisite, precise gold and tumbaga technical metalwork. Artifacts associated with this culture are heavily characterized by poporo design and anthropomorphic representations.

Artifacts like this container showcase the sophisticated metallurgical skills developed in the pre-Hispanic Americas. The alloy used—often a mix of gold and copper known as tumbaga—allowed artisans to achieve delicate details and warm, lustrous surfaces through gilding techniques.

These containers often held lime used during ceremonial coca leaf chewing rituals, serving both functional and deeply symbolic cultural roles within ancient Colombian societies.
